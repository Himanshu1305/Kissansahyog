## 0. Quick answer: "NIRF" vs "NIF"

- **NIRF is the National Institutional Ranking Framework.** Wikipedia says it is "a ranking methodology released annually by the Ministry of Education, Government of India, to rank institutions of higher education in India". It was launched on 29 Sep 2015, and the first rankings came out in 2016. It has nothing to do with grassroots innovation. [Secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutional_Ranking_Framework
- **NIF is the National Innovation Foundation – India.** This is the correct body for jugaad and grassroots innovators. It is "An Autonomous Institute of Department of Science & Technology", established in March 2000. [Official] https://nif.org.in/aboutnif
- On the live page, use "NIF (National Innovation Foundation – India)" and never "NIRF".

## 1. Support ecosystem

### 1.1 National Innovation Foundation – India (NIF)
**Source:** [Official] https://nif.org.in/aboutnif, https://nif.org.in/public/, https://nif.org.in/biennial_campaign, https://dst.gov.in/autonomousstinstitutions/national-innovation-foundation

- **What it is.** NIF was set up in March 2000 under DST, Government of India, and is headquartered in Gandhinagar, Gujarat. Its mission is to help "India evolve into a creative and knowledge-based society by expanding the policy and institutional space for grassroots technological innovators."
- **Its services, by department (nif.org.in/aboutnif):**
  - **Scouting & Documentation (SDDM).** NIF has "compiled an extensive database of over 3,75,000 technological ideas, innovations, and traditional knowledge practices" from 730+ districts.
  - **Value Addition & R&D (VARD).** NIF has "facilitated the validation and/or value addition of several hundred grassroots technologies". It runs a Fabrication Laboratory and a Herbal Analytical Laboratory.
  - **IP Management.** NIF has filed "over 1,614 patent applications". 735 patents have been granted in India and 5 in the USA. It also holds 24 design registrations and 11 trademark applications.
  - **Business Development & MVIF.** The "Micro Venture Innovation Fund (MVIF)" is supported by SIDBI. It has backed 238 innovation projects, and 28 of the resulting enterprises are DPIIT-recognised start-ups.
  - **Dissemination.** NIF reports commercialisation "across six continents" and 120 technology-licensing cases.
- **Public database.** innovation.nif.org.in lists 139,584 innovations. It says it is "purely an informative website". [Official] https://innovation.nif.org.in/
- **15th National Biennial Competition (current).**
  - Scope: "Green Grassroots unaided Technological Innovations, Ideas & Outstanding Traditional Knowledge".
  - Deadline: "The last date of receiving entries is March 31, 2027." The home page says entries are "open till 31st March 2027".
  - Categories include agriculture, health and veterinary, energy and household, transport and livestock, green or low-cost technology, traditional knowledge, and creative ideas (a prototype is not required).
  - Eligibility: Indian citizens. People who are pursuing or hold technology graduate or postgraduate degrees, professional science or technology courses, or an M.Sc. are excluded, as are R&D professionals. Employed applicants need a No Objection Certificate. Note that this excludes many polytechnic or engineering graduates as applicants.
- **How to submit.**
  - Online form: https://nif.org.in/public/submitidea (separate student and grassroots routes).
  - Email: campaign@nifindia.org
  - Post: National Innovation Foundation – India, Grambharti, Amrapur, Gandhinagar–Mahudi Road, Gandhinagar, Gujarat 382650.
  - Phone: 02764-261131 / 32 / 34 / 35 / 36 / 38 / 39. Contact page: https://nif.org.in/public/contact_us
- **Other programmes.** The DST page (older figures) mentions the Festival of Innovations at Rashtrapati Bhavan, IGNITE for school children (now the "Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam IGNITE" awards), and Presidential awards.

## 2. Legal issues for innovators

### 2.1 Patents Act, 1970: novelty and the public-listing trap
Text taken from Indian Kanoon [Secondary-mirror]. Official source: ipindia.gov.in or indiacode.

- **s.2(1)(j) "invention"**: "a new product or process involving an inventive step and capable of industrial application". https://indiankanoon.org/doc/65643/
- **s.2(1)(ja) "inventive step"**: "a feature of an invention that involves technical advance as compared to the existing knowledge or having economic significance or both and that makes the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art".
- **s.2(1)(l) "new invention"**: "any invention or technology which has not been anticipated by publication in any document or used in the country or elsewhere in the world before the date of filing of patent application with complete specification".
- **Grace provisions are narrow:**
  - **s.29**: protects only publication made *without the inventor's consent*, and only if the inventor then files as soon as reasonably practicable.
  - **s.31**: protects only display at an industrial or other exhibition (notified by the government), publication arising from that display, or a paper read before a learned society. The application must follow **within twelve months**. https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1937976/
  - **s.32**: protects public working in India "within one year before the priority date" by the inventor, and only "for the purpose of reasonable trial".
  - None of these covers an ordinary online sale listing.
- **What this means for Kisan Sahyog.** If a maker posts photos, a video or a description showing *how* the jugaad works, and offers it for sale, the invention can lose patent novelty. This is the single most important caution for the page.
  - Recommend that makers either (a) show what it does, not how it is built (no close-up mechanism photos or drawings), or (b) first talk to NIF or file a provisional application.
- **Cost.** The official filing fee for natural persons and start-ups is ₹1,600 (up to 30 pages and 10 claims). [Secondary] https://ssrana.in/ufaqs/patent-application-filing-registration-cost-india-inr-1600/ (confirm on ipindia.gov.in).

### 2.2 Designs Act, 2000 (shape and appearance)
- **s.4.** A design shall not be registered if it "is not new or original", or "has been disclosed to the public anywhere in India or in any other country by publication in tangible form or by use or in any other way prior to the filing date", or is "not significantly distinguishable from known designs". [Secondary-mirror] https://lawgist.in/designs-act/4
- The same public-listing risk applies. Example: Mitticool holds a design registration (see §5).

### 2.3 Trade Marks Act, 1999 (product name)
- **s.27(1)**: "No person shall be entitled to institute any proceeding to prevent, or to recover damages for, the infringement of an unregistered trade mark."
- **s.27(2)** preserves passing-off actions. [Secondary-mirror] https://lawgist.in/trade-marks-act/27
- **Advice.** A maker who wants to stop copycats using a product name ("Bullet Santi"-style) should consider registration. Makers must not use other companies' brand names (Mahindra, Royal Enfield and so on) as their product name.
- Platform angle: under IT Rules r.3(1)(b), content that infringes a trademark is prohibited content (§3.4).

### 2.4 Dangerous Machines (Regulation) Act, 1983 (Act 35 of 1983, 14 Dec 1983)
Source [Secondary-mirror]: https://bnblegal.com/bareact/the-dangerous-machines-regulation-act-1983/ ; summary https://blog.ipleaders.in/all-you-need-to-know-about-the-dangerous-machines-regulation-act-1983/ . The official PDF at indiacode https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/18454/1/1._the_dangerous_machines_(regulation)_act,_1983.pdf timed out.

- **Scope.** A "dangerous machine" means "a power thresher, and includes any such machine intended to be used in the agricultural or rural sector" that may cause death or injury to operators. The Centre can notify more machines.
- **Machines covered by the 2007 Rules:** power threshers, power-operated chaff cutters and sugarcane crushers.
- **Manufacturer and dealer duties** (Act plus Rules):
  - Conform to the prescribed IS standards.
  - Guard the prime-mover and transmission parts with "safeguard of substantial construction".
  - Mark "danger signals indicating the point beyond which no limb shall be inserted".
  - Mark rotation direction, rpm and power requirement.
  - Supply "a manual containing general instructions regarding the operation of such machine, in Hindi and regional languages".
  - Give a written declaration of compliance on transfer.
- **Owner and operator duties** (iPleaders summary): registration with the Controller, no child operators, first aid on hand, and reporting accidents within 3 days.
- **Penalty** (bnblegal mirror): for a first offence, up to 6 months' imprisonment and/or a fine up to ₹1,000.
- **Commencement.** Some provisions apply in a State only when notified. Whether MP has notified them was **not verified**.
- **Use on the page.** Any jugaad thresher, chaff cutter or cane crusher listed on Kisan Sahyog falls squarely in this regime. Say plainly that the maker must follow these safety requirements.

### 2.5 Consumer Protection Act, 2019: product liability (Chapter VI, ss.82–87)
Text from advocatekhoj / Indian Kanoon mirrors [Secondary-mirror].

- **s.2(7) "consumer"** covers "offline or online transactions through electronic means…".
- **s.2(17) "electronic service provider"**: "a person who provides technologies or processes to enable a product seller to engage in advertising or selling goods or services to a consumer and includes any online market place". https://indiankanoon.org/doc/47873513/
- **s.2(34) "product liability"**: "The responsibility of a product manufacturer or product seller… to compensate for any harm caused to a consumer by such defective product…"
- **s.2(36) "product manufacturer"** includes a person who "makes any product or parts thereof; or assembles parts thereof made by others", puts their own mark on others' products, or "designs, produces, fabricates, constructs or re-manufactures any product before its sale". **A jugaad maker is a product manufacturer.**
- **s.2(37) "product seller"**: "a person who, in the course of business, imports, sells, distributes, leases, installs, prepares, packages, labels, markets, repairs, maintains, or otherwise is involved in placing such product for commercial purpose".
  - The exclusion only helps a person who "is not a manufacturer, wholesaler, distributor, retailer, direct seller or an electronic service provider". In other words, **an electronic service provider is not automatically outside "product seller"**. This is a residual risk for the platform. Disclaimers, passivity and not "placing" the product all help.
- **s.83**: "A product liability action may be brought by a complainant against a product manufacturer or a product service provider or a product seller…"
- **s.84 (manufacturer)**: liable if there is "a manufacturing defect", it is "defective in design", there is deviation from specifications, it fails an express warranty, or it "fails to contain adequate instructions of correct usage… or any warning". Under s.84(2) the manufacturer is liable "even if he proves that he was not negligent".
- **s.86 (seller who is not the manufacturer)**: liable only if it "exercised substantial control over the designing, testing, manufacturing, packaging or labelling", "altered or modified the product", made its own express warranty, sold where the manufacturer is unknown or cannot be served, or "failed to exercise reasonable care in assembling, inspecting or maintaining…".
  - **Platform takeaway.** Do not certify, warrant, test-label, modify or "approve" jugaads. Always show the maker's identity, because the seller's liability is triggered when "the identity of product manufacturer… is not known".
- **s.87.** No action against the seller if the product "was misused, altered, or modified". The manufacturer is not liable for failure to warn about a danger "which is obvious or commonly known".

### 2.6 Motor Vehicles Act, 1988: s.52 and jugaad vehicles
- **s.52(1)**: "No owner of a motor vehicle shall so alter the vehicle that the particulars contained in the certificate of registration are at variance with those originally specified by the manufacturer". The provisos allow approved alternate-fuel kits (battery, CNG, LPG and so on) and Centre-prescribed retrofits or exemptions. [Secondary-mirror] https://www.indianconstitution.in/2021/07/section-52-motor-vehicles-act.html
- **Regional Transport Officer & Ors. v. K. Jayachandra & Anr.** [Official text on Indian Kanoon; Press summary on SCC Online]
  - Court and date: Supreme Court of India, Civil Appeal Nos. 219–222 of 2019, decided 9 Jan 2019. Bench: Arun Mishra and Vineet Saran JJ.
  - Holding: "No vehicle can be altered so as to change original specification made by manufacturer." Alterations allowed by the Rules are permissible "except as prohibited by section 52". Particulars originally specified by the manufacturer in the RC cannot be varied.
  - https://indiankanoon.org/doc/22832301/ ; https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2019/01/09/original-specifications-made-by-the-manufacturer-of-the-vehicle-cant-be-altered-sc/
- **Chairman, Rajasthan SRTC & Anr. v. Smt. Santosh & Ors.** (SLP(C) No. 3265 of 2012; Supreme Court, 10 May 2013; Dr B.S. Chauhan and F.M. Ibrahim Kalifulla JJ). This is **the key "jugaad" ruling.**
  - "as the `Jugaad' is covered in the definition of the motor vehicle under Section 2(28) of the Act, the statutory authorities cannot escape from their duty to enforce the law and restrain the plying of `Jugaad'."
  - Para 32: authorities may exempt "if such a vehicle is exclusively used for agricultural purposes but for that sufficient specifications have to be provided so that it cannot be used for commercial purposes."
  - https://indiankanoon.org/doc/100267722/ ; summary [Secondary] casemine.com
- **Bijender Singh v. Raj Kumar & Ors.** (Punjab & Haryana HC, Pankaj Jain J., 27 Mar 2025)
  - "Merely for the reason that the claimant was driving a Jugaad vehicle without there being any overt act attributable which led to cause of accident, the Tribunal ought not have held the appellant negligent."
  - Compensation was enhanced to ₹3.5 lakh. [Press] https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/punjab-and-haryana-high-court/punjab-haryana-high-court-compensation-for-accident-cant-be-denied-because-claimant-was-driving-jugaad-vehicle-without-registration-number-288481
- **Platform rule (recommended).** Do not accept listings of road-going jugaad vehicles (modified motorcycle or tractor-trolley carriers, peter-rehda and similar) that are offered for road or passenger use. Allow field-only machines with a "not for road use" declaration.
  - Bullet Santi-type devices work in the field. If a listing uses a registered motorcycle's engine, the maker should state that it is not to be driven on public roads unless registered as required.

### 2.7 Electrical safety: CEA (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations, 2023
Notification No. CEA-PS-16/1/2021-CEI, 8 Jun 2023 [Official] https://cea.nic.in/wp-content/uploads/notification/2023/06/pdf_100_183_English.pdf . Regulation numbers below come from automated extraction; verify against the PDF before citing them.

- **Reg. 14(1)**: lines and apparatus must be "constructed, installed, protected, worked and maintained in such a manner as to ensure safety of human beings, animals and property."
- **Reg. 31(1)**: no electrical installation work "shall be carried out… except by an electrical contractor licenced in this behalf by the State Government… under the direct supervision of a person holding a certificate of competency."
- **Reg. 43**: the frame of every motor and similar apparatus "shall be earthed by two separate and distinct connections with earth".
- **Reg. 44**: installations to be controlled by a residual current device. In domestic installations the device must not exceed 30 mA.
- The PDF has no specific electric-fence regulation. For electric jugaads (motor-driven cutters, pumps, solar or electric fencing), advise: proper earthing, an RCD/ELCB, a licensed electrician for wiring, and no direct-from-mains fencing.

## 3. Platform protection

### 3.1 IT Act, 2000: intermediary and safe harbour
[Secondary-mirror: Indian Kanoon https://indiankanoon.org/doc/844026/ ; Wilmap https://wilmap.stanford.edu/node/29571 ; indiacode PDF https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/13116/1/it_act_2000_updated.pdf]

- **s.2(1)(w) "intermediary"**: "any person who on behalf of another person receives, stores or transmits that record or provides any service with respect to that record and includes … web-hosting service providers, search engines, online payment sites, online-auction sites, **online-market places** and cyber cafes".
- **s.79(1)**: "an intermediary shall not be liable for any third party information, data, or communication link made available or hosted by him."
- **s.79(2)**: this applies if the intermediary does not "initiate the transmission", "select the receiver" or "select or modify the information contained in the transmission" (s.79(2)(b)), **and** "the intermediary observes due diligence while discharging his duties under this Act and also observes such other guidelines as the Central Government may prescribe" (s.79(2)(c)).
- **s.79(3)(b)**: protection is lost if, "upon receiving actual knowledge, or on being notified by the appropriate Government or its agency…", the intermediary "fails to expeditiously remove or disable access to that material".
- **Design implications:**
  - Do not edit sellers' technical claims.
  - Moderate by removing listings, not by rewriting them.
  - Keep the original listing text and a log.

### 3.2 Shreya Singhal v. Union of India
- Supreme Court, 24 Mar 2015. W.P. (Crl.) No. 167 of 2012 and connected matters. R.F. Nariman and J. Chelameswar JJ. AIR 2015 SC 1523. Full text: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/110813550/ [Official text]
- **Holding** [Secondary, SFLC]: the court "read down Section 79 of the IT Act and held that the 'actual knowledge' requirement for an intermediary to take down content has to be read to mean either an intimation in the form of a court order or on being notified by the government." https://sflc.in/policies-and-cases/shreya-singhal-v-union-of-india/
- The court also struck down s.66A.
- **Practical reading.** A private complaint alone does not create s.79(3)(b) "actual knowledge". However, the IT Rules 2021 separately require a grievance mechanism and timelines (§4), and failing those due-diligence duties loses safe harbour under Rule 7.

### 3.3 E-commerce platform cases (Delhi HC)
- **Christian Louboutin SAS v. Nakul Bajaj & Ors.** (Delhi HC, CS(COMM) 344/2018, Prathiba M. Singh J., 2 Nov 2018) [Official text on Indian Kanoon] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/99622088/
  - The platform (Darveys) loses s.79 protection when it becomes an "active participant".
  - Factors include: identifying and promoting sellers, guaranteeing authenticity, logistics, invoicing in its own name, collecting payment, and using trademarks in meta-tags.
  - "When an e-commerce website is involved in or conducts its business in such a manner, which would see the presence of a large number of elements enumerated above, it could be said to cross the line from being an intermediary to an active participant."
- **Amazon Seller Services Pvt. Ltd. v. Amway India Enterprises Pvt. Ltd. & Ors.** (Delhi HC Division Bench, FAO(OS) 133/2019; S. Muralidhar and Talwant Singh JJ.)
  - Date: the judgment PDF header reads "31 January, 2020" [Press-hosted PDF] https://images.assettype.com/barandbench/2020-02/456c90bd-a81e-4cc4-8f35-c22b833072f4/Amazon_vs_Amway__ors.pdf . LiveLaw (report dated 1 Feb 2020): https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/amazon-v-amway-direct-selling-guidelines-not-binding-e-commerce-platforms-may-sell-products-of-dses-with-prior-permission-read-judgment-152255
  - Holdings [Press]: the Direct Selling Guidelines are not "law" ("Merely because the DSGs are notified in the Gazette, they do not attain the status of 'law'"). The single-judge injunction was set aside.
  - On s.79 [Secondary, Wilmap/IndiaCorpLaw], the Bench is reported to have held that "there is no distinction between passive and active intermediaries so far as the availability of the safe harbor provisions are concerned". The exact paragraph was **not seen in the judgment text**, so treat this as a secondary characterisation.
- **Kisan Sahyog lesson.** Stay at the passive end of the Louboutin factors:
  - no "verified/approved" badges implying quality,
  - no price setting,
  - no payments or logistics,
  - no guarantee of genuineness,
  - no promotion of particular sellers' unsafe items.
  - The soft "we may try to share your innovation with colleges" language is fine, provided it is framed as information-sharing and not endorsement.

### 3.4 IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, as amended through Feb 2026
[Official] MeitY text incorporating the 2026 amendment: https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/550681ab908f8afb135b0ad42816a1c9.pdf ; earlier consolidated version: https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Information-Technology-Intermediary-Guidelines-and-Digital-Media-Ethics-Code-Rules-2021-updated-06.04.2023-.pdf

- **Rule 3(1), opening**: "An intermediary, including a social media intermediary, a significant social media intermediary and an online gaming intermediary, shall observe the following due diligence". This applies to **every** intermediary, with no size threshold.
- **Rule 3(1)(a)**: "prominently publish on its website… the rules and regulations, privacy policy and user agreement in English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule". Hindi qualifies.
- **Rule 3(1)(b)**: inform users and make reasonable efforts against hosting prohibited content. The list includes content that infringes "patent, trademark, copyright or other proprietary rights" and content that violates law.
- **Rule 3(1)(d)**, on court or government order: remove "within **three hours**" (was 36 hours).
- **Rule 3(2)**: the Grievance Officer duties; see §4.
- **Rule 3A**: appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee "within a period of thirty days".
- **Rule 7**: "Where an intermediary fails to observe these rules, the provisions of sub-section (1) of section 79 of the Act shall not be applicable to such intermediary and the intermediary shall be liable for punishment".
- **2026 amendment.** G.S.R. 120(E), dated 10 Feb 2026 [Official MeitY PDF]. Press reports give 20 Feb 2026 as the effective date [Press: chambers.com; Secondary: iPleaders https://blog.ipleaders.in/it-rules-2026/]. Changes:
  - r.3(1)(d): 36 h → **3 h**
  - r.3(2)(a)(i) resolution: 15 days → **7 days**
  - removal-request proviso: 72 h → **36 h**
  - r.3(2)(b) intimate imagery or impersonation: 24 h → **2 h**
  - The 24-hour acknowledgement is unchanged.
  - The amendment also adds synthetic-media (AI) labelling duties.
- The MeitY text also contains an Explanation: "'prominently publish' shall mean publishing in a clearly visible manner on the home page of the website or the home screen of the mobile based application". Show the Terms, Privacy and Grievance link on the home page and in the global footer.
- **Precedent on consequences** [Press]: in Amit Acharya v. Union of India (Delhi HC; hearing of 5 Jul 2021), MeitY told the court that Twitter's non-compliance with the IT Rules 2021, including not appointing a resident grievance officer, meant "the non-compliance is leading to Twitter losing its immunity". https://www.livelaw.in/news-updates/twitter-stripped-of-safe-harbour-immunity-under-it-act-delhi-high-court-non-compliance-176910 . That case concerned the extra Rule 4 duties of a significant social media intermediary, but the Rule 7 consequence is the same for all intermediaries.

### 3.5 Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020: do they apply?
[Secondary-mirror] https://www.legitquest.com/act/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020/91C8 ; https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/Consumer_Protection_E-Commerce_Rules_2020.pdf . Official: consumeraffairs.nic.in, notified 23 Jul 2020; the official page timed out.

- **Rule 2(1)** applies to "(a) all goods and services bought or sold over digital or electronic network including digital products" and "(b) all models of e-commerce, including marketplace and inventory models."
- **"E-commerce entity"**: "any person who owns, operates or manages digital or electronic facility or platform for electronic commerce…"
- **"Marketplace e-commerce entity"**: "an e-commerce entity which provides an information technology platform on a digital or electronic network to facilitate transactions between buyers and sellers".
- The Act's s.2(16) defines "e-commerce" as "buying or selling of goods or services including digital products over digital or electronic network".
- **Rule 4(4)**: "establish an adequate grievance redressal mechanism", "appoint a grievance officer", and "display the name, contact details, and designation of such officer on its platform".
- **Rule 4(5)**: acknowledge "within forty-eight hours" and redress "within one month from the date of receipt".
- **Rule 4(1)(a)**: requires a company (or foreign company) and a nodal officer resident in India.
- **Rule 5**: marketplace duties include s.79 compliance and seller undertakings on accurate descriptions and images.
- **Analysis (not a ruling).**
  - On Kisan Sahyog, no buying or selling happens "over digital or electronic network": no order, cart or payment. Contact is by phone or WhatsApp and the deal is concluded offline.
  - There is therefore a good argument that it is a classifieds or listing service, not an "e-commerce entity". The Rule 4(1)(a) company requirement also suggests the Rules target transacting businesses.
  - Against that, the definitions use "facilitate transactions" and "advertising" (CPA s.2(17)), and no court or CCPA ruling on free classifieds was found.
  - **Conservative course.** Satisfy Rule 4(4)–(5) anyway. The IT-Rules timelines (24 h acknowledgement, 7 days resolution) are stricter than 48 h / 1 month, so one officer meeting the IT Rules also meets these.
  - Also adopt Rule 5-style seller undertakings: accurate description and photos, and the maker's name, location and contact.

### 3.6 DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025 (phone numbers are personal data)
[Official mirror] Act: https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf . Rules: G.S.R. 846(E), 13 Nov 2025 [Secondary-mirror of gazette text] https://www.dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf ; PIB note of 14 Nov 2025 [Official] https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190655

**The Act**
- **s.5(1)**: every consent request "shall be accompanied or preceded by a notice… informing her,—(i) the personal data and the purpose for which the same is proposed to be processed…"
- **s.6(1)**: consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action".
- **s.8(9)**: "A Data Fiduciary shall publish… the business contact information of a Data Protection Officer, if applicable, or a person who is able to answer on behalf of the Data Fiduciary…"
- **s.8(10)**: "A Data Fiduciary shall establish an effective mechanism to redress the grievances of Data Principals."
- **s.13**: a right to grievance redressal, which must be exhausted before going to the Board.
- **Penalties** (Schedule): up to ₹250 crore.

**The Rules**
- **Commencement (Rule 1):**
  - Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 apply on publication.
  - Rule 4 (consent managers) after one year.
  - **Rules 3, 5–16, 22–23 "eighteen months after the date of publication"**, which is about **13 May 2027** [Secondary: privybyidfy].
- **Rule 3 (notice)**: must be "understandable independently of any other information", with "an itemised description of such personal data" and "the specified purpose", plus links for withdrawing consent and exercising rights.
- **Rule 9**: "prominently publish on its website or app" the contact details of the DPO or an equivalent person.
- **Rule 14**: grievances answered "within a reasonable period not exceeding ninety days".

**For Kisan Sahyog**
- The seller's phone number is published on purpose, so the notice must say so (purpose: buyers contact you).
- Add a consent checkbox, a way to delete a listing or account, and a named contact (who can be the same person as the Grievance Officer).
- The legal deadline is May 2027, but doing it now costs nothing.

## 4. Specific question: why do we need a Grievance Officer?

### (a) Does IT Rules 2021, Rule 3(2) bind every intermediary, whatever its size or revenue?
**Yes.**
- Rule 3(1) opens with "**An intermediary**, including a social media intermediary, a significant social media intermediary and an online gaming intermediary, shall observe the following due diligence". Nothing in Rule 3 depends on revenue, users, payments or fees. The user-count threshold (5 million users for a "significant social media intermediary") triggers only the *extra* Rule 4 duties.
- **Rule 3(2)(a)** [Official MeitY text]: "The intermediary shall prominently publish on its website, mobile based application or both, as the case may be, the name of the Grievance Officer and his contact details as well as mechanism by which a user or a victim may make complaint…"
- **Current timelines** (as amended by G.S.R. 120(E), 10 Feb 2026):
  - **r.3(2)(a)(i)**: "acknowledge the complaint within twenty-four hours and resolve such complaint within a period of **seven days** from the date of its receipt" (previously 15 days).
  - **Proviso**: a request to remove content in the prohibited categories of r.3(1)(b) "shall be resolved within **thirty-six hours** of such reporting" (previously 72 h).
  - **r.3(2)(a)(ii)**: "receive and acknowledge any order, notice or direction issued by the Appropriate Government, any competent authority or a court of competent jurisdiction".
  - **r.3(2)(b)**: content showing nudity or sexual acts, or impersonation including morphed or deepfake images, must be removed within **two hours** of a complaint (previously 24 h). This is unlikely on a farm marketplace but must be stated.
  - **r.3(1)(d)**: court or government takedown orders within **three hours** (previously 36 h).
  - **r.3A**: users can appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee within 30 days.

### (b) Is a website that hosts user listings an "intermediary"?
**Yes.**
- IT Act s.2(1)(w): "any person who on behalf of another person receives, stores or transmits that record or provides any service with respect to that record and includes… **online-market places**".
- Kisan Sahyog stores and displays sellers' listings on their behalf. Being free and off-platform does not change this. The definition turns on hosting third-party records, not on money.

### (c) Do the E-Commerce Rules 2020 add a grievance officer requirement?
**They might, though applicability is arguable.**
- Rule 4(4)–(5) require an e-commerce entity to display "the name, contact details, and designation" of a grievance officer, with acknowledgement within 48 h and redress within one month.
- Whether a free listing site with offline deals is an "e-commerce entity" is debatable (§3.5), and no ruling was found.
- Complying with the IT Rules timelines automatically meets these, so it costs nothing extra.

### (d) Does the DPDP Act require a contact person?
**Yes.**
- s.8(9) requires publishing the business contact of a DPO "or a person who is able to answer on behalf of the Data Fiduciary".
- s.8(10) requires a grievance mechanism.
- Rule 9 requires that contact to be prominently published, and Rule 14 sets a reply within 90 days or less.
- Rules 9 and 14 take effect about 13 May 2027.

### (e) Minimum compliant implementation, and the risk of skipping it
**Minimum setup**
- One section titled "शिकायत अधिकारी / Grievance Officer", linked from the footer of every page including the home page. Put it on the Contact page and refer to it from the Terms and Privacy Policy.
- It should contain:
  - **Name**: a real person's name, for example the founder or any team member (Rule 3(2)(a) says "the name of the Grievance Officer"; Rule 4(4) of the E-Com Rules also asks for "designation").
  - **Designation**: "Grievance Officer & Data Protection Contact, Kisan Sahyog".
  - **Email**: a role address is fine, for example shikayat@kissansahyog.com, plus a postal address. A WhatsApp number is optional.
  - **How to complain**: a simple Hindi form or email, quoting the listing URL or ID.
  - **Commitments**:
    - acknowledgement within 24 hours;
    - resolution within 7 days;
    - unlawful-content removal requests acted on within 36 hours;
    - sexual or impersonation content removed within 2 hours;
    - court or government orders actioned within 3 hours;
    - data-privacy requests answered within 7 days (well inside the DPDP 90-day ceiling).
  - The Grievance Appellate Committee route (https://gac.gov.in), with the 30-day appeal window.
  - The date the page was last updated.
- **Internal practice**:
  - Log every complaint (date, time, action, reply).
  - Keep removed listings and data as evidence.
  - Make sure someone checks the inbox daily, because the 2 h and 3 h windows are very short.

**Can the officer just be a designation, or any team member?**
- Any team member, including the founder, can be named. Rule 3 sets no seniority or qualification requirement for an ordinary intermediary.
- A designation with **no name** does not match the text ("the name of the Grievance Officer").
- A residency requirement is stated expressly for significant social media intermediaries (Rule 4). For ordinary intermediaries it was not seen in Rule 3, but naming an India-based person is the safe choice.

**"We can only remove the listing" is fine**
- Resolution can mean removing the listing, editing nothing, rejecting the complaint with reasons, or referring the person to the police or court.
- The Rules do not require Kisan Sahyog to refund, mediate money or decide disputes between buyer and seller.

**Risk of not having it**
- Under Rule 7, failure to observe the Rules means "the provisions of sub-section (1) of section 79 of the Act shall not be applicable", and the intermediary is "liable for punishment".
- Without safe harbour, Kisan Sahyog could be sued or prosecuted as if it had published each third-party listing. Examples: an unsafe chaff-cutter that injures someone, a copied patented design, or a defamatory or fraudulent post.
- MeitY took this exact position against Twitter in 2021 (§3.4).
